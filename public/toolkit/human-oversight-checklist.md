# Human oversight checklist (EU AI Act Art. 14)

Use for every person assigned to oversee a high-risk AI system. Art. 14(4) lists what overseers must be enabled to do.

**System:** [name] · **Overseer:** [name, role] · **Training completed:** [date]

## The overseer can…
- [ ] **Understand** the system's capabilities and limits, and monitor its operation, including spotting anomalies and unexpected performance.
- [ ] **Stay aware of automation bias**: the tendency to rely too much on the system's output, especially for recommendations.
- [ ] **Interpret** the output correctly, using the tools and explanations available.
- [ ] **Decide not to use** the output, or **disregard, override or reverse** it, in any particular situation.
- [ ] **Intervene or stop** the system with a stop button or similar procedure that brings it to a safe state.

## Arrangements
- [ ] Overseers have the competence, training and authority to do this (Art. 26(2)).
- [ ] Workload allows real review, not rubber-stamping: [cases per hour, time per case].
- [ ] Overrides are logged with a reason, and override rates are reviewed: [frequency].
- [ ] For remote biometric identification: no action unless two trained people have confirmed the match (Art. 14(5)).
